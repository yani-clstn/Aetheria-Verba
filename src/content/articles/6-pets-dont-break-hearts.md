We spend so much of our lives learning to guard ourselves against human love. 

We learn to read between lines that were never written, to watch for quiet distance in a room, and to prepare for the inevitable shift when someone decides we are no longer worth holding onto. Human heartbreak is complex. It carries words left unsaid, doors slammed shut, or worse—a slow fading out into strangers.

Perhaps that is why I love animals the way I do.

An animal does not know how to play cold to get your attention. They do not hold grudges over yesterday’s bad mood, nor do they measure their affection based on whether you earned it that day. Their love is simple, raw, and completely unreserved.

When you sit beside them, you are entirely safe. There is no betrayal waiting in the shadows. No hidden motives, no sudden change of heart.

The only time an animal will **ever break your heart is when theirs stops beating.**

And even that heartbreak, heavy as it is, carries no malice. It is simply the price we pay for the privilege of being loved by something so pure. It is the final lesson they leave behind: that love was worth every single quiet moment, right up until the very end.
