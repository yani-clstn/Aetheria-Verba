There is an unwritten rule that comes with being the eldest daughter: you are born into a role that requires you to figure things out first on your own.

You become the default third parent, the organizer, the "takbuhan" ni younger brother. You learn how to guide younger siblings through things you had to navigate entirely alone. You become expert at holding your composure, absorbing expectations, and carrying responsibilities before you even fully understand what adult stress actually feels like.

People see *Ate* as strong, reliable, independent, and capable of handling everything on her own. 

What they often forget is that *Ate* was once just a child, too. She had no older sister to lean on when her heart was heavy, no kuya to protect her from sexual threats, no safety net to catch her when she stumbled, and no blueprint on how to grow up without making mistakes along the way. She had to learn by trial, error, and quiet resilience, or maybe, she had to accept and choose practicality over passion.

When the eldest daughter breaks, she usually does so in secret—behind closed doors, late at night, or in silent moments when everyone else is already asleep. Because she feels responsible for keeping the peace, she rarely gives herself permission to be vulnerable.

So be gentle to *Ate*. Walang ate si ate. She went through things she didn't talk about. She made that "practicality" her passion. She is doing it all scared, she chooses to do everything with passion.

Be patient with her overthinking, or the moments when she carries the world like it’s solely her job to keep it spinning. Remember that behind all that independence is simply someone who had to grow up a little too fast—and who rarely gets to be taken care of the way she takes care of everyone and everything else.

After all, *walang Ate si Ate.*

*Love, Ate.*
