You become the default third parent, the organizer, and the *takbuhan* of your younger brother. You learn how to guide your siblings through situations you had to navigate entirely alone. You become an expert at holding your composure, absorbing expectations, and carrying heavy responsibilities before you even fully understand what adult stress actually feels like.

People see *Ate* as strong, reliable, independent, and capable of handling everything on her own. 

What they often forget is that *Ate* was once just a child, too. 

She had no older sister to lean on when her heart was heavy, no *kuya* to shield her from threats, no safety net to catch her when she stumbled, and no blueprint on how to grow up without making mistakes along the way. She had to learn through trial, error, and quiet resilience—often having to choose practicality over passion.

When the eldest daughter breaks, she usually does so in secret—behind closed doors, late at night, or in the quiet moments when everyone else is already asleep. Because she feels responsible for keeping the peace, she rarely gives herself permission to be vulnerable.

So be gentle to *Ate*. 

*Walang Ate si Ate.* She went through things she never talks about. She forced herself to make practicality her passion. And even while doing it all scared, she still chooses to lead with love.

Be patient with her overthinking, or the moments when she carries the world like it is solely her job to keep it spinning. Remember that behind all that independence is simply someone who had to grow up a little too fast—and who rarely gets to be taken care of the way she takes care of everyone else.

After all, *walang Ate si Ate.*

*Love,*  ***Ate***
